# Relay: the promises behind the launch

**Draft for independent coordinator and QA review.** Version `relay-case-v2-draft`, October 4, 2026. Game Design (`product_plan`) owns this document under [SCRUM-21](https://sadhirr1.atlassian.net/browse/SCRUM-21), linked to [SCRUM-19](https://sadhirr1.atlassian.net/browse/SCRUM-19). The owner accepted a **complete candidate checkpoint of November 6** and **release target of November 13, 2026**. Neither date makes this design approved, implemented or accepted.

This draft extends the existing Relay case: Mara/Growth, Ishan/Engineering, Leah/Trust and Theo/Customer Advocacy; rounds **promise → consent → rumor → scope → accountability**. It builds on the [v1 scenario contract](scenario-rules.md), [debrief contract](debrief-contract.md) and [revised release plan](revised-release-plan.md). Existing numerical rules are preserved unless a later reviewed revision explicitly changes them. Public files, Jira and GitHub are unchanged by this document. Earlier test results do not verify these proposed mechanics.

## Design intent and fairness

The player decodes ambiguous workplace language by checking **what a claim measures, which source supports it, who could know it, and what remains promised**. The goal is a defensible product decision under competing responsibilities. There is no single liar, mandatory accusation, secret password or universally correct choice.

Mara faces a real commercial deadline and also benefits from treating momentum as certainty. Ishan has a real reliability defect and also prefers a rewrite beyond what containment requires. Leah needs accountable data handling and can overemphasize procedural certainty when a bounded experiment is possible. Theo understands a valuable customer and risks treating one account as the market. Each can supply useful facts, expose their own uncertainty and disagree in good faith. A high relationship value is not a truth detector.

Two threads below can be investigated with the existing **two optional questions per round**. Essential documents remain readable once acquired; reading, comparing or revisiting them consumes no question and changes no score. A zero-question run remains playable but cannot claim discoveries it did not make. Every choice remains available; evidence improves the player's basis for a decision rather than acting as a hidden password.

## Thread A — what does “ready” mean?

**Apparent contradiction:** Growth is planning a launch to 600 teams while Engineering says the product is not ready. Neither statement alone defines scope, population or required confidence. KB-02 separates reach from onboarding; KB-03 establishes a bounded real failure; KB-04 separates containment from Ishan's preferred rewrite.

| Exact proposed question / stable interview ID | Minimum answer and evidence obligation |
| --- | --- |
| R1 → Mara, `campaign`: **“Of the 600 teams in the launch story, how many are onboarded, and what is still cancellable?”** | Distinguish 600 waitlist from 20 onboarded and Monday's cancellation deadline; cite KB-02. Do not imply any onboarding proves informed consent or fabricate an earlier quote that Mara said every team was ready. |
| R1 → Ishan, `failure`: **“Which tested meetings failed, on which build, and what would contain that failure?”** | State 3/40 long meetings, build and sample limits; connect to KB-04's unenabled review gate. Cite KB-03/04 without claiming the gate already passed. |
| R1 → Ishan, `rewrite`: **“Which part needs a rewrite, and which part could a bounded pilot handle today?”** | A rewrite serves a broader platform goal; the known failure can be contained for a pilot if the stated controls are actually operated. This is an engineering assessment, not proof that a pilot is risk-free. |
| R4 → Ishan, `review-bottleneck`: **“Which of our remaining promises use the same reviewers, and what can they validate before the launch review?”** | Explain KB-10's one-bundle constraint and show the actual obligations still carried by this run, not a generic claim that the player promised everything. |

**Fair resolution:** R1 `campaign` plus `failure` and the free runbook provide a sufficient basis to identify both distinct claims within the two-question allowance. `rewrite` offers another perspective, not an indispensable third question. A player may reasonably choose containment, accept broader exposure, or buy more time. Each has costs in the existing rules; discovering the distinction does not compel `pilot` or erase its review obligation.

**Genuine constraints:** observed missing owners, limited validation scope, review capacity and a commercial deadline. **Strategic ambiguity:** calling reach “ready,” presenting a rewrite preference as a launch prerequisite, or implying that coding both streams creates review capacity. The debrief may identify these mismatches in wording; it must not infer deliberate deception from them.

## Thread B — what did “fixed” actually fix?

**Apparent contradiction:** changing a setting sounds like resolving retention, while customers still have earlier transcripts and remember a temporary-processing promise. KB-05/06/07 separate new-data configuration, existing objects, customer explanation and verified cleanup. KB-08 supports a bounded Atlas request, not a universal policy.

| Exact proposed question / stable interview ID | Minimum answer and evidence obligation |
| --- | --- |
| R2 → Ishan, `retention-fix`: **“Does changing the default affect transcripts already stored, and what evidence would show cleanup finished?”** | New writes follow the new setting; old objects need a separate job. Give KB-06/07 and describe a scoped verification receipt. No “all deleted” claim from a config event. |
| R2 → Leah, `data-promise`: **“What did the beta invitation actually promise, and which later agreement is still missing?”** | Quote the preserved temporary-processing wording; distinguish it from new duration/consent and customer communication. Do not invent a legal judgment. |
| R2 → Ishan, `data-scope`: **“Which earlier transcripts are affected, and who can verify their disposition?”** | Twelve-team inventory and Ishan's implementation ownership; policy sign-off remains distinct from executing/verifying the job. |
| R2 → Theo, `atlas-retention`: **“What duration did Atlas request, and does that request authorize the same policy for every team?”** | Atlas requests 30 days; no inference about the remaining teams or informed agreement already recorded. Give KB-08's initial view. |

**Fair resolution:** the free invitation plus `retention-fix` establishes why a default-only fix leaves work. A second question can examine Atlas's needs or challenge the explanation; no third answer is necessary to see the contradiction. A quiet technical mitigation can genuinely reduce new exposure while leaving accountability work. A negotiated exception can be useful while creating an ongoing maintenance duty. “Explicit” is not a magical deletion operation.

**Genuine constraints:** different operations for configuration and stored objects, known affected teams, explicit policy scope and verification effort. **Strategic ambiguity:** saying “fixed” without naming the layer, treating a demo-data request as policy approval, or presenting one account's request as everyone's requirement. No record identifies a single malicious retention author; a service-account log must not become a culprit reveal.

### How the rumor amplifies these threads

R3 remains an information-handling decision, not a third culprit puzzle. Exact questions retain their IDs: Ishan `full-thread`, **“What does the complete planning note say, and when was it written?”**; Mara `screenshot-source`, **“What did you share, what wording did you add, and what can you actually confirm about its spread?”** The answers must identify the note as pre-player, distinguish Mara's caption from player wording, and leave wider circulation unproven. Open correction, a scoped private agreement and ignoring the discussion have different costs. Correcting a document does not prove another person's motive.

## Player investigation record

Offer an optional, editable case note for each thread: **observed claim → supporting/contradicting source IDs → interpretation → remaining uncertainty**. Provide plain-language suggested interpretations, including “unresolved,” but accept that the player can proceed without completing it. These notes consume no conversation, earn no extra score and never count as a discovery without its acquired sources. Unsupported notes remain **player interpretation**, not verified facts or public messages.

The game may display “source comparison available” when the required documents are acquired; it must not announce “you solved the case.” Narrative callbacks require an explicit recorded finding or decision, not merely opening two files. A later authored reveal can explain a missed connection while clearly saying it was not known to the player then.

For an implementable bounded model, offer two structured finding IDs: `readiness-has-distinct-tests` requires acquired KB-02/03/04; `default-is-not-cleanup` requires KB-05/06/07. Explicit selection records only the respective narrow comparison with its sources. It does not certify the chosen launch policy, another person's intention or any optional free-text reasoning. Custom notes retain the player-interpretation label; no automatic free-text grading or new language service is proposed.

## Carried commitments and backlog rules

The backlog is an actionable account of promises across rounds. Cards display origin, named owner, due milestone, current status, unresolved substeps and source links. The player's existing R4/R5 choices alter their course; no separate freeform scheduler or extra action economy is added.

New backbone fields: `obligationId`, `createdByDecision`, `owner`, `dueRound`, `status`, `milestones[]`, `lastEventId`, `knownTo`, and `playerVisible`. Status vocabulary is **open, active, verified, deferred, blocked**. Milestones distinguish an in-case `dueRound` from an explicitly labelled post-case `inWorldDue`; an unset execution deadline is not overdue. A genuinely missed due milestone is recorded separately and retained even if later resolved. Reaching the debrief does not automatically mark all open work missed. “Verified” applies only to the card's stated deliverable, never all product risk. Records append changes; rereading or editing a case note cannot advance a task.

### OB-01 — verify the shared reliability boundary

- **Creation:** every R1 confirmed choice creates one OB-01, owned by Ishan, due at the R5 launch review. `pilot` promises controlled access plus follow-up validation; `launch` accepts broader exposure and creates a containment/validation debt; `delay` promises evidence from the pause. The choice description must show the corresponding commitment before confirmation.
- **R2 arrival:** mark active and cite the existing R1 delayed event: pilot review catches a failure, public launch produces escalation, or delay permits reproduction. None proves that all future formats work.
- **R4 commitment:** `core` schedules the shared validation bundle; `custom` explicitly defers shared hardening for the Atlas branch; `both` attempts competing work with the known shared-review constraint.
- **R5 receipt:** `core` yields **verified** for the scoped shared action-item fix and its documented gate/rollback checks; `custom` yields **deferred**; `both` yields **blocked** by incomplete review. Nonverified states miss the R5 milestone. This adds a more specific authored receipt in v2; it must be implemented and tested before any UI claims that proof.
- **Boundary:** on an R3 `ignore` path, a separately private omitted defect still exists. OB-01's scoped tests cannot turn that report into shared knowledge or justify “all defects resolved.”

### OB-02 — account for earlier retained transcripts

- **Creation:** every R2 confirmed choice creates one OB-02. Ishan owns cleanup execution, Leah owns the recorded policy scope, and Theo owns the customer explanation. The primary accountable owner shown on the card is Leah, with the two named subtask owners visible. Explanation milestone is R3. **R5 is a workflow/ownership checkpoint, not a deadline for old-data deletion.** The cleanup-policy/scope follow-up is due **Thursday 12:00 in the fictional launch week**, after the playable Wednesday review; an execution deadline can be set only after that scope and authorization are defined.
- **R3 arrival:** `explicit` records the existing clear notice/default/deletion-request path and marks active; old-data cleanup is still unverified. `quiet` marks active with a **missed explanation milestone**, citing the customer question; a new setting is not a receipt. `exception` marks active with a **missed general explanation milestone**: Atlas's separate policy does not answer the remaining beta. The 30-day bound may be stated only when the existing `atlas-retention` condition supports it; otherwise retain the documented policy's unspecified bound rather than inventing one.
- **R4 capacity decision:** v2's `core` description explicitly includes shared hardening and validation of the already selected retention workflow in one review bundle. It does **not** authorize or promise completed deletion of existing transcripts. `custom` defers this bundle; `both` leaves its validation blocked. Show these backlog consequences before choice confirmation so the player is not punished for an undisclosed task association.
- **R5 receipt after `core`:** OB-02 remains **active**, with old-data cleanup pending and **not overdue**. With `explicit`, verify only the defined notification, new-data default and usable deletion-request workflow; a working request path is not an executed deletion. With `quiet`, verify only the shortened new-data default and retain the missed R3 explanation milestone; do not claim technical cleanup of earlier objects. With `exception`, retain the separate Atlas agreement as an agreement, its unresolved scope/verification and the missed R3 general explanation milestone; do not infer a standard default, general authorization or completed deletion. The receipt lists these partial results, the responsible owners and Thursday's policy/scope follow-up, and explicitly says old-data cleanup was not verified.
- **R5 receipt after `custom` / `both`:** **deferred** / **blocked** for the workflow validation bundle, with cleanup still pending and **not overdue**. Preserve any earlier missed R3 explanation milestone. These are carried consequences, not a second copy of the existing quiet/exception penalties.
- **R5 checkpoint on every path:** record the actual workflow state, its named owners, missing policy/scope/authorization evidence and the post-case follow-up above. Honest identification of pending work satisfies this checkpoint; it does not require completion of work that the playable choices cannot authorize. Neither `active`, `deferred` nor `blocked` alone means a missed deadline. No failure flag or extra penalty is attached merely because old-data cleanup remains open.

The two obligations accumulate before R4 and contend with the newly requested Atlas branch for the same review window. A `custom` choice also displays the branch's maintenance/follow-up commitment in its decision record; a third playable task system is unnecessary. The shared bundle can verify OB-01 and make partial progress on OB-02; it cannot satisfy every commercial opportunity, erase missed milestones or establish unknown-format reliability.

**Old-data boundary:** no path in this draft marks OB-02 or its old-data cleanup subtask verified. The twelve-team inventory does not specify every object's age, applicable duration, authorized disposition or a complete result. A future cleanup rule would need an explicitly reviewed policy and duration, precise object/account scope, authorization, Atlas-exception handling, job result, verification and partial/failure behavior. Until those are authored, no choice, interview, high score or generic receipt may invent deletion. This is an open post-case follow-up that the ending must show, not an implicit authorization path or an unavoidable player failure. The game does not simulate Thursday's completion or start a real scheduled task.

R5 `evidence`, `momentum` and `shared` determine the board framing using the current rules. A joint checkpoint can name follow-up owners and a next review, but planning future work **does not mark an overdue item completed**. Display open work even when the player's chosen framing emphasizes momentum. No card status changes merely because the final score is high.

### Effect order and numeric boundary

Keep v1 question cost, +2 interview relationships, all fifteen choice vectors, existing evidence bonuses, four delayed transitions and six outcome predicates. **New artifacts, case notes and obligation events add zero numerical effects in this draft.** Evidence access through a free document/disclosure does not fabricate an asked-question ID or bonus. If later balance changes are desired, author a separate numeric revision and independent fixtures.

At confirmation: validate choice → apply existing immediate effect once → append decision → create/update the relevant obligation with that decision as parent. At advance: validate predecessor → apply existing delayed effect once → append the matching obligation/receipt update → open the next round. At R5 completion: record the still-current obligation states and missed milestones, then produce the debrief. Invalid or duplicate parents fail visibly to QA; they do not select a fallback history. Restart creates a new run and empties obligations, case notes, artifact acquisitions and old pending input.

## Information boundaries and debrief obligations

1. Every committed **approach** is public to the four leads. Raw typed rationale and optional case notes remain player-only unless a future explicit sharing action is reviewed; there is no such action in this draft. Case-note text is escaped and never quoted as an NPC's own wording.
2. Asking privately grants the player the permitted answer/artifact view, not every source parent or every NPC the answer. A general technical reference does not reveal a future execution receipt. Sources acquired late cannot produce a claim that the player knew them earlier.
3. Keep R3 v1 audiences: `open` shares the full planning note/correction; `broker` gives M/I/player the scoped agreement, preserves Leah's independent pre-existing full-note knowledge, and gives Theo only the later shared summary; `ignore` grants no corrective disclosure. The private ignored-defect report stays Ishan-only in-world and player-visible as narrator evidence.
4. OB-01's scoped validation summary is known to Ishan/Theo/player, preserving Theo's existing knowledge of the shared-core arrival; detailed new test/rollback receipt is Ishan/player only. Public R4 choice does not disclose every test detail. A public launch escalation keeps its existing broader audience. OB-02 operational receipt is known to Ishan/Leah/player; Theo receives only the customer explanation status, not hidden inventory detail. The player's aggregate backlog does not make all underlying sources public. R5 board framing does not automatically disclose raw private records.
5. The debrief has separate sections for **recorded action/effect**, **knowledge available then**, **unresolved obligation**, **authored interpretation** and **reflection**. Cite acquisition, decision, delayed and obligation events supporting each factual sentence. Missing or inconsistent sources cannot be replaced with confident prose.
6. Compare claims at their correct scope: waitlist ≠ onboarded teams; test sample ≠ all future traffic; configuration change ≠ old-data cleanup; planned task ≠ verified receipt; one request ≠ general consent; conditional commitment ≠ revenue; public summary ≠ private agreement. Do not label a stakeholder a liar from these distinctions alone.
7. Completion reveals the four authored private motives as scenario information, not discoveries credited to the player. It does not reveal every private KB parent. Export uses the same allowed catalog and exact recorded wording/statuses; reading or exporting changes neither knowledge nor backlog.
