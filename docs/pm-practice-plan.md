## Product purpose

Help aspiring and practising product managers develop domain knowledge, investigate ambiguous information, make defensible decisions and communicate them under interview pressure. The product is a practice platform spanning PM roles. An individual exercise is a unit of practice, not the product boundary.

Werewolf contributes partial information, private incentives, competing claims, questioning, persuasion and commitments. Beautiful, usable web design supports these interactions. Photoreal characters, a fictional world and a fixed story are not dependencies.

Both update one skills profile. An interview attempt can recommend a general-practice drill; completing that drill can inform the preparation path. ## Role model and exercise coverage

Build preparation from domain + product responsibility + seniority + JD evidence + interview stage. Common PM skills are shared; technical material and assessment expectations differ. Seniority changes scope, independence and organisational complexity as well as difficulty.

The table illustrates content design, not verified questions asked by employers.

| Role or specialisation | Knowledge and judgement to practise | Werewolf-style pressure | Evidence and assessment focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform / API PM (bounded practice available) | Developer needs, reliability, adoption, compatibility and dependencies | A major customer requests an exception that competes with shared-platform health | Assess downstream costs, user impact and migration options; negotiate priorities. |
| Growth PM (bounded practice available) | Funnels, experiment design, retention, business value and guardrails | A headline conversion gain conflicts with retention or support evidence | Check denominators, segments and alternative explanations; weigh longer-term effects. |
| UI / UX-focused PM | Research, flows, accessibility, frontend constraints, usability and outcome metrics | Design preference, user feedback and conversion numbers point in different directions | Distinguish preference from evidence; inspect user segments, accessibility and measurement; propose a useful test. |
| AI / ML PM (later coverage) | Evaluation, data quality, latency/cost trade-offs, failure handling | A benchmark claim hides a narrow test set; another stakeholder overstates integration readiness | Ask about evaluation scope, failure cost and operational constraints; avoid unsupported technical claims. |
| ADAS / automotive (optional example) | System dependencies, operating conditions, user needs, validation and release judgement | Commercial and engineering claims describe different slices of readiness; test evidence has limited coverage | Identify scope limits, ask for relevant validation, explain dependencies and a defensible release recommendation. Subject-matter review is required before presenting this as expert preparation. |

Reusable exercise families include diagnosis, prioritisation, estimation, technical trade-offs, evidence investigation, negotiation and decision defence. Do not produce role coverage merely by replacing nouns in the same question.

## How the Werewolf mechanics work

1. **Brief and information distribution.** The learner receives an objective and visible constraints. Simulated participants have public responsibilities, private incentives and defined knowledge. Claims can be accurate, mistaken, stale, partial or deliberately misleading. A round need not contain a liar.
2. **Investigation.** Ask natural-language questions, request records, compare claims and consult a domain reference. Technical knowledge determines which questions are useful. Guided mode offers support; assessment mode records assistance separately.
3. **Deliberation.** Form a proposal, explain assumptions and hear objections. Optional investigation limits create pressure only when the exercise explains them. A beginner can practise without a timer.
4. **Commitment.** Choose a priority, verification step, investment or recommendation. Show its resource and information consequences before confirmation. A simulated council can accept or reject an argument; that response does not determine the learner's competence score.
5. **New evidence and response.** Reveal an authored or validated event linked to the exercise state. Let the learner revise, investigate further or defend the existing choice. Record why the position changed.
6. **Debrief and retry.** Reveal relevant hidden information after the round. Reconstruct what was available at each decision, assess reasoning fairly and offer a different exercise addressing the same skill.

This is initially solo practice with simulated stakeholders. Multiplayer, human adversaries and a competitive deception mode are later possibilities, not first-release requirements. Conventional interview questions and focused technical drills remain available within both paths; deduction is used when it serves the learning objective.

Stable facts and knowledge access belong to the exercise rules. A dialogue model may express a participant's permitted claim, including an authored false claim, but cannot silently rewrite the underlying facts, leak another participant's secrets or invent a new correct answer. Unsupported questions receive a useful limitation or clarification. Participant disagreement must not automatically signal dishonesty.

## Feedback and learning quality

Assess problem framing, domain accuracy, evidence use, trade-offs, calibrated uncertainty, adaptation and communication. Use dimension-specific examples and anchors; weight dimensions by the confirmed role. Feedback must cite the learner's actual statements/actions and the information available at that time. A lucky outcome or persuasive accusation alone earns no competence credit. Multiple well-supported answers can pass.

Separate recall/knowledge checks from judgement evaluation. Identify rubric uncertainty and allow a learner to challenge feedback. Real employer questions require provenance; otherwise label material as original practice. Never describe AI feedback as validated hiring prediction.

Each domain pack needs source references, a review date, exercise facts, participant knowledge, allowed events, several defensible answer paths, scoring anchors and independent review. Domain reviewers must be identifiable real reviewers; assigning an agent a specialist label does not establish subject expertise. Technical accuracy in the software-product exercises selected for release is a dependency owned by Product. Optional specialist examples do not set the release priority.

## Low-usage implementation approach

Work in manually initiated bounded increments. Use one author and one independent reviewer for a small deliverable, rotating the seven specialist roles as needed; do not activate seven agents for routine changes. Stop after an inspectable increment and a concise handoff.

Reuse appropriate existing state/evidence/accessibility ideas only after a source audit; existing prototypes do not establish a suitable platform foundation. Preserve historical work and isolate new code. No art generation or broad visual polishing during planning.

Keep reviewed facts, rubrics, scoring rules and state transitions local where possible. Use prepared samples to develop the UI. Cache approved material and JD analysis by version; do not repeatedly send the full conversation to a model. Use AI only where open-ended interpretation/dialogue adds value. Measure per-session inference usage and enforce a server-side monetary limit when a provider/budget is approved. Show an honest fallback when that limit is reached. Coding-agent usage and the application's model charges are separate; this plan promises neither a percentage saving nor free AI operation.

JD text is private input: preview it before any external processing, minimise retention and allow deletion. Real JDs, resumes, session transcripts and keys must stay out of the public repository and analytics logs. Use synthetic fixtures for development. Treat uploaded text as data, never as application instructions; prevent hidden-rule disclosure and cross-user leakage.
